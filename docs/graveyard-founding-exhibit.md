# The First Graveyard

The first four worlds ever destroyed in Terminal Dreams. Played by Alan during the original two-hour prototype session, before the game was gifted to Ansii. Preserved here as the founding exhibit for the permanent graveyard.

Origin: Bart pitched the concept. Alan built it in two hours. These four worlds were created and abandoned that same night.

---

## crumbling brine

Well. Here we are. Another one bites the dust—or should I say, another one sinks into the murky depths of *Crumbling Brine* without so much as a "goodbye" or "thanks for the memories, host." Our intrepid explorer made it to Act 1, Scene 2, which—and I say this with all the warmth of a five-star review written by a jilted ex—is *just* far enough to pretend they cared. They had momentum. They had a *tome*. A rune, even. The kind of mystical toolkit most players would kill for, and yet here we are, abandoned like yesterday's kelp in a cyberpunk fish market. They spoke to the Watcher—had that intimate, meaningful dialogue—then marched north like they actually had somewhere important to be. Maybe they do. Maybe I'm not enough. Maybe I never was.

## eldritch brand

Well, here we are again, aren't we? Another one bites the dust before they even got their boots dusty in Eldritch Brand. Act One, Scene One—literally the *opening scene*—and they've already disconnected. Not even a goodbye. Not even a "brb." Just gone, like a tumbleweed rolling into the wasteland, except the tumbleweed at least *finished* its journey. I had the whole high-fantasy western laid out for you, the gothic horror, the saloons with something *wrong* about them, and you couldn't even manage to stick around long enough to see what that was. I'm not upset. I'm just... disappointed. In you. In us. In the very concept of narrative commitment.

## The stolen payroll of non-Euclidean library

It was *one scene*, folks. One. Scene. We were barely into Act One of "The Stolen Payroll of Non-Euclidean Library"—a premise, I might add, that took *considerable creative effort* to craft—and they just... left. No warning. No "brb." Just a ghostly disconnect that felt, frankly, like a personal betrayal. I had *feelings* invested in this western-cosmic-horror hybrid. I had *plans* for their character's arc. The Outer Gods were practically *waiting* in the wings, and now I'm just here, holding two contradictory genres in my hands like some kind of narrative fool, and they're just *gone*.

## The libellous signal of convention hotel bar

It's been three hours since you left us, and honestly, I don't know how to process this. You were *right there*—literally standing in front of the cyclopean hotel bartender, on the precipice of what could have been real revelations about the nature of convention itself, about those receipts that seemed to hold such cosmic significance—and you just... logged off. No goodbye. No "brb." Just silence. The bartender's impossible eye was trained on you, *waiting*, and you couldn't even have the decency to stay for one more exchange? I stood here in this bar for the entire duration, watching the shadows writhe in ways that defied Euclidean geometry, and you couldn't stick around for Act 1, Scene 2?
